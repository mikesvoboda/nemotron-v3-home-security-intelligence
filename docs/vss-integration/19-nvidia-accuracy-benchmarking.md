## 1. The answer

(1) For the 3.x line, NVIDIA does not publish an accuracy measurement of the VSS alert-verification pipeline. Its "Performance" section is sizing only (latency, throughput, streams per GPU), and the repo's accuracy tooling is a set of LLM-judge harnesses on internal data, with no published results and no pass/fail gate [E]. (2) What NVIDIA publishes: the only quantified VLM accuracy figure in the 3.x docs is one relative delta. EVS++ pruning cut VLM latency about 20% for about 1% lower accuracy, on an unnamed "warehouse dataset" with Cosmos 3 Super Reasoner FP8, and the metric, n and interval are undefined [E]. The docs otherwise call BF16 "the tested variant" and warn of hallucinations at FP8/NVFP4. The other accuracy numbers are CV-model KPIs (HOTA, AP@0.50) and a 2.3.1 to 2.4 blog table (LongVideoBench 48.17 to 68.32%, MLVU 61.24 to 71.44%) with no methodology [E]. (3) What the repo's code does: two harnesses ship under skills/benchmarking. The caption harness is a paired baseline/candidate regression. Its ground truth is gpt-4.1 output, it uses one claude-opus-4-8 judge, and its verdict is sign-only. NVIDIA states its own noise floor as about 0.01 per scene on repeat runs and up to 0.05 across sessions [E]. The Q&A harness runs on the DSS-internal dataset vss-devx-base and reports a mean 0-1 judge score. Its only published number is "~0.465 baseline" in a troubleshooting bullet [E]. Neither harness is in CI, neither has a threshold, and neither implements a confidence interval or significance test [E]. (4) The legacy NAT report/QA/trajectory evaluators are being deprecated, and the live docs page still shows placeholder scores (0.9/0.8/0.89) and names no dataset. The skill-eval harness checks whether a coding agent can deploy VSS, not VLM accuracy [E]. (5) Model-level numbers (not VSS-specific): the closest NVIDIA analogue to our verify gate is VANTAGE-Bench Event Verification. It has n=163 (104 positive, 59 negative), is zero-shot with greedy decoding, and reports macro F1 plus sensitivity/specificity and 95% bootstrap CIs (EV median ±7.47) [E]. Among the 7 models with a published sensitivity/specificity split, the best specificity is 76.27%. That is 14/59 false accepts, 23.7% (Wilson 14.7-36.0) [C]. Cosmos3-Nano has no split in Table 7; its EV macro F1 is 68.88 ±7.16 [E]. (6) Not published: absolute precision, recall or false-alarm rates for the shipped verifier, accuracy by precision, refusal rates, calibration, search or summarization quality, any CI on a VSS figure, human-labelled ground truth for VSS VLM benchmarks, home-security or night/IR footage, and any GeForce-class result. Whether Cosmos 3 training data (1K internal tailgating-verification clips) overlaps VANTAGE EV (50 tailgating items) is also not stated. (7) Our bars are stricter and better instrumented than anything NVIDIA publishes for VSS, but VANTAGE is a different task. NVIDIA's numbers therefore neither validate nor refute S2 <= 5%. Thin spots: the absences rest on greps over docs, repo and blogs as of 2026-10-03 (develop d6a8095a, live docs 3.2.1), and NVIDIA's private pipelines and talks were not visible.

**Confidence.** Medium-high on the central finding that no VSS-level accuracy measurement of the alert verifier is published for 3.x. It was checked independently against the repo at develop HEAD d6a8095a14b7692c52bfcfe08afe925c058d01f0 (2026-10-03), the live docs (banner 3.2.1), the performance pages, release notes and GitHub releases, ten developer.nvidia.com and four blogs.nvidia.com posts, and forum posts. High on the mechanics of the caption and Q&A harnesses, which were read at code level (the VANTAGE numbers were read directly from the paper's tables). Moderate on every 'not published' statement, because each rests on keyword/grep searches over docs, repo, blogs and forum posts. Gaps: (1) NVIDIA-internal material is invisible (the private downstream GitLab pipeline, the internal DSS dataset, any internal accuracy reports, and GTC/Computex session content). (2) The Cosmos3 Super card (JS shell), Cosmos3-Nano/Super-Reasoner HF repos (401) and the Cosmos-Reason2-8B card (gated) could not be read, so BF16-only and per-variant statements are verified for Nano only. (3) A legacy VSS 2.4.1 alerts harness with TP/FP/FN scoring and an example Cosmos-Reason1 result sheet was reported by track readers, but was not confirmed in the claim-verification pass, so it is treated as an unverified lead. (4) The implied false-accept rates and Wilson intervals are our own computation from VANTAGE Table 7 (counts reconcile with the published macro F1) and cover only the 7 models in that table. (5) VANTAGE and TAR numbers are model-level benchmarks that do not mention VSS. All reads are dated 2026-10-03, and the audited commits cdad5cc0e and 1e94133b4 have the same evaluator code and accuracy-related docs as HEAD, except renames, version bumps and placeholder files.

### RT-VLM alerting / captioning throughput

- **How measured.** Throughput test of a single Yes/No output token (OSL=1) and of 100-token captioning. It is not a test of verdict correctness.
- **Datasets.** None. Synthetic load: 10 s chunks, 80 frames at 448x448 (7,840 vision tokens).
- **Metrics.** Max concurrent streams, chunk E2E avg/p90/p95, GPU utilisation. The page advises 10-15% headroom below the maximum.
- **Harness.** docs/performance-rt-vlm.mdx (VSS 3.2, Cosmos3 Nano Reasoner FP8, vLLM; H100, RTX Pro 6000 SE, DGX Spark, AGX Thor).
- **Reported numbers.** H100 streaming: alerting max 51 streams, chunk E2E avg 3.6 s / p90 4.86 / p95 4.95; captioning (OSL=100) 33 streams, avg 4.78 s.
- **Source.** docs/performance-rt-vlm.mdx:23-58,229 @ d6a8095a [E]

- **Harness.** None published. The only FP8 accuracy datapoint is the EVS++ one (Super FP8, pruning on, no BF16 comparison).
- **Reported numbers.** No hallucination rate or accuracy delta for any precision.

### Captioning / VLM regression (skills/benchmarking/vss-evaluate-caption-accuracy)

- **How measured.** Paired regression test, not an absolute benchmark: capture REF (no change) and HYP (change) captions over the same videos in one session, score both against a ground truth with an LLM judge, and report the accuracy delta plus time saved. The question it answers is 'did this RT-VLM change make captions worse?'. Ground truth is gpt-4.1 output (60 frames/chunk) versus 20 frames/chunk for REF/HYP.
- **Datasets.** User-supplied surveillance videos mapped in a SCENES dict (warehouse, traffic, hospital, office; 10 s chunks). None are shipped and the report table is hard-wired to 6 scenes. No home-security footage. Runs used Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct.
- **Metrics.** Judge prompt v2: 5 axes scored 0-1, plus entity/event tp/fp/fn lists with a partition invariant. Deterministic critical-event and interaction F1 (type/relation and |dt| <= 5 s). Combined score is the mean of the axis macros. Verdict is sign-only (tie margin default 0.0). Chunk order is randomized per judge to reduce position bias.
- **Harness.** gt/capture/judge/table stages. Shipped judge is claude-opus-4-8 through the local claude CLI (single judge). Multi-judge code exists but run_eval.sh suppresses it. nemotron-vl is excluded after 'pilot runs'. Not in CI and no tests. A code comment says GT uses a free-form prompt, but the code runs GT/REF/HYP with the same structured prompt.
- **Reported numbers.** No scores committed. Stated noise: repeat runs of an identical config span about 0.01 on one scene and the REF/HYP delta has changed sign. Judging against a REF captured in another session moved per-scene deltas by up to 0.05. No run count or CI.
- **Source.** skills/benchmarking/vss-evaluate-caption-accuracy/{SKILL.md:19-24,43-46,78-80,118-143; scripts/run_captioning.py:99-103,215-235,699-703; scripts/multi_judge.py; scripts/score.py:299-320; scripts/aggregate_table.py:47-48; skill-card.md:19,25} @ d6a8095a [E]

### Video Q&A (skills/benchmarking/vss-benchmark-vlm-qa; replaces NAT QA path)

- **How measured.** Host-side script calls `vss vlm run` against a deployed Cosmos Reason 3 RT-VLM (default --num-frames 20) and scores each answer with an OpenAI-compatible LLM judge (temperature 0.0, think-then-score). Rubric: yes/no exact, counts exact, timestamps within +/-5 s, partial credit allowed. The headline is the mean of per-item scores, so it is a mean graded score and not a proportion correct. Errored or killed items are recorded as errors (score None, excluded from the mean, exit 3), never as a low score.
- **Datasets.** vss-devx-base on the NVIDIA Dataset Service (DSS), about 290 MB, internal (NGC key or SSO plus tenant/group). Layout: dataset_single_turn.json, dataset_multi_turn.json, 4 report GT files, 7 videos. Question count and label provenance are not stated.
- **Metrics.** accuracy_mean plus latency mean/p50/p90/p95/p99 in summary.json (judge_model and model_served recorded). No CI. No threshold: exit 0 = no item errored, exit 3 = a download or item error.
- **Harness.** benchmark_vlm_qa.py. 42 unit tests cover the harness, not model accuracy. Not wired into CI. It benchmarks RT-VLM through the CLI and involves no CA-RAG. The docs warn that the judge moves absolute scores and must be held fixed across compared runs.
- **Reported numbers.** '~0.465 baseline' in a troubleshooting bullet only: no n, judge model, GPU, date or CI. The 'published baselines' the SKILL refers to were not found.
- **Source.** skills/benchmarking/vss-benchmark-vlm-qa/{SKILL.md:21-24,96-102,141-146,184-185; scripts/benchmark_vlm_qa.py:49-106,481,706-746,946-959}; deploy/docker/developer-profiles/dev-profile-base/eval/README_eval.md:14-15,57,118-138; PR #1963 merged 2026-09-04 [E]

### Agent Report / QA / Trajectory evaluators (NAT `nat eval`, legacy)

- **How measured.** Framework on NeMo Agent Toolkit with three evaluators. Report: bottom-up field -> section -> report scoring with exact_match, token f1, regex, non_empty, llm_judge. QA: semantic 0-1 judge. Trajectory: tool selection and parameters. The user supplies the dataset and ground truth. A judge failure after retries becomes score 0.0 inside the mean, which differs from the new QA harness.

### Model-level (NOT VSS): VANTAGE-Bench Event Verification (arXiv 2609.09396)

- **How measured.** Binary hypothesis verification on fixed-camera clips, scored by macro F1 (sklearn classification_report) with sensitivity and specificity reported separately. Zero-shot, greedy (temperature 0), chain-of-thought off, fps 1-4, max 256 frames, 16.7e6 pixels, on H100 80GB (Palmetto 2) plus cloud. Uncertainty is a 95% percentile bootstrap over items (10,000 resamples); the paper says the intervals cover item sampling only, not prompt or decoding changes. Seed SD is below 0.5 (Qwen3-VL-8B, 3 seeds). The scorer takes the first standalone yes/no anywhere in the output and drops outputs with none (printed as 'Valid Predictions n/N' but not in the returned metrics), so it cannot expose a refusal rate.
- **Datasets.** EV n=163 (104 positive, 59 negative): metropolis-EV 67, tailgating 28+22, warehouse near-miss 46. Real SF/Dubuque and licensed footage; EV is not the synthetic part. Elevated fixed cameras 8-20 ft; eye-level and low-angle views not covered; extreme weather and lighting not systematically covered. GT withheld, scored on a server leaderboard; the Cosmos eval-kit needs internal S3/DSS.
- **Metrics.** Macro F1; sensitivity; specificity.
- **Harness.** Clemson-Capstone/VANTAGE-Bench @3a428aec; NVIDIA/Cosmos vlmevalkit.
- **Reported numbers.** Macro F1 / sens / spec: Gemini 3.6 Flash 82.00/87.50/76.27; GPT-5.6 Sol 76.14/77.88/76.27; Cosmos-Reason2-32B 73.58/83.65/62.71; Cosmos3-Super 71.28/72.12/72.88; Gemini 3.1 Pro 68.57/71.15/67.80; Cosmos-Reason2-8B 64.09/71.15/57.63; Qwen3-VL-8B 59.39/50.96/74.58. Cosmos3-Nano EV macro F1 68.88 ±7.16 (not in the split table). EV median CI half-width ±7.47. [C] Implied false-accept rates: 14/59=23.7% (Wilson 14.7-36.0), Cosmos3-Super 27.1% (17.4-39.6), CR2-32B 37.3% (26.1-50.0), CR2-8B 42.4% (30.6-55.1).
- **Source.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09396 Tables 1, 7, 12, 13, 14, App. F-G; vlmeval/dataset/vantage_event_verification.py:92-99,220-263 @3a428aec; https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/PhysicalAI-VANTAGE-Bench (sha ad5297f6); NVIDIA/Cosmos README.md:27,154 [E][C]

### Model-level (NOT VSS): Cosmos 3 report and TAR-Bench

- **How measured.** Cosmos 3 Reasoner is evaluated on 48 benchmarks in 4 groups (VLMEvalKit + vLLM) and reported as single point values, with no CIs and no stated decoding, thinking mode or hardware. The model card ships its tables as PNG images. TAR-Bench BCQ is plain Yes/No accuracy by regex on a balanced 160-item set, with open-ended tasks scored by BERTScore F1 and an unweighted mean. TAR reports mean±std over 3 runs, which is not item-level uncertainty.
- **Datasets.** Smart Infrastructure group = 8 VANTAGE tasks + TARBench. TAR-Bench: 960 human-curated items, 80 clips from 17 YouTube videos, 10 tasks. TAR training labels are mostly VLM pseudo-labels (910 of 3,670 videos had human annotations as context). Cosmos 3 training includes 1K internal surveillance clips for tailgating verification; overlap with VANTAGE's 50 tailgating EV items is not stated.
- **Metrics.** Group averages; EV macro F1; BCQ accuracy.
- **Harness.** Cosmos 3 tech report sec 5.4 and 6.1; TAR paper arXiv 2608.10317 sec 4.1.
- **Reported numbers.** Table 10 (Nano/Super/Edge): General 69.6/73.7/59.7; Smart Infra 61.0/62.6/47.9. Cosmos 3 report EV: Super 71.3, Nano 68.9, Edge 64.8, CR2-32B 73.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro 67.5. The same cells differ from the VANTAGE paper (Gemini 3.1 Pro 68.57; Edge 63.36), so cells are not version-stable. TAR BCQ: Gemini 3.1 Pro 64.4, Qwen3.5-27B 72.5, CR2-32B 67.7, CR2-8B 47.7, Qwen3-VL-8B 59.6; Cosmos3-Nano 75.8 and Super 89.8 are TAR-exposed in pretraining. [C] n=160 gives Wilson half-width about ±7.7 at p=0.5.

## 3. What NVIDIA does not publish (searched, not found)

- Any absolute accuracy, precision, recall or false-alarm rate for the shipped VLM alert verifier (Alert Bridge or RT-VLM) on a named labelled set, in the 3.x docs, release notes, GitHub releases or blogs. The only quantity is the relative EVS++ statement (about 1% lower accuracy, unnamed warehouse dataset, Super FP8, metric and n undefined).
- Any before/after measurement of the false-positive reduction that is the stated purpose of VLM verification. 'Improved Near Miss Violation verification accuracy through prompt tuning' (release-notes.mdx:223) has no metric; a track reader also reports an 'order of magnitude' false-positive claim in alerting-service.mdx:1335 with no data.
- Accuracy by weight precision. BF16 is the tested variant, FP8/NVFP4 may hallucinate, and no per-precision accuracy table exists. For Cosmos3 Nano the model card says only BF16 is tested, and the Super card was not readable.
- A refusal, unparseable-output or verification-failed rate. Docs acknowledge that a valid 'rejected' can be stored as verification-failed but give no rate. The VANTAGE scorer silently drops outputs without a yes/no word.
- Calibration or risk-score quality (ROC/PR, reliability, threshold sweeps). The verdict is binary; 'risk score' has 0 hits in VSS at 1e94133b4, and NVIDIA's verification benchmarks score one hard Yes/No operating point.
- Retrieval accuracy for the Search profile (recall@k, mAP, HIT@1; only latency/QPS on 'vad-r1-v2' are published) and summarization or CA-RAG quality (LVS page is latency only). Whether the 17 search-benchmark clips are held out from the embedder's Vad-Reasoning fine-tune is not stated.
- Any detail behind the '~0.465' Q&A baseline (n, judge model, GPU, date, CI), and the methodology behind the LongVideoBench/MLVU 2.3.1 to 2.4 table. The vss-devx-base dataset is internal to NVIDIA (DSS), so outsiders cannot reproduce NVIDIA's QA runs.
- Any confidence, bootstrap or Wilson interval on any VSS accuracy figure. The only variance language is the caption skill's observed noise floor (about 0.01 repeat, up to 0.05 cross-session). Bootstrap CIs appear only in the VANTAGE paper, which is not VSS.
- Human-annotated ground truth for VSS VLM benchmarks. Caption GT is gpt-4.1 output; QA/report GT provenance is unstated. No judge validation was found in the code read (no human-agreement study or kappa); the only internal evidence is the 'nemotron-vl excluded after pilot runs' note.
- Home-security, residential, doorbell, night/IR or low-light results. VANTAGE states eye-level views are not covered and extreme weather/lighting is not systematically covered. All published VSS and VANTAGE accuracy is warehouse, traffic or smart-infrastructure style.
- Any GeForce-class (RTX 4090/5090) accuracy result. VSS perf pages cover RTX PRO 4500/6000, H100, DGX Spark and AGX Thor; VANTAGE ran on H100. The RTX 4090 skill-eval tables are empty and concern agent deploys, not VLM accuracy.

## 4. How their approach compares with our bars

Where our setup is stronger. (1) S2 and S3 are pre-declared hard bars (5% and 90%, owner-set) with n and a 95% Wilson interval per cell and 'insufficient' under n=10. VSS publishes no threshold or interval on any accuracy figure. The caption harness uses a sign-only verdict, the Q&A harness never gates on score, and VANTAGE reports bootstrap intervals only on macro F1 [E]. (2) Truth is declared at generation and its own error rate is measured by the 60-still owner audit. NVIDIA's VSS ground truth is gpt-4.1 output for captions and unstated for Q&A, and VANTAGE/TAR GT is withheld, so nobody outside can audit it. (3) We replay through the shipped VlmClient on the target consumer card and track S1 (VRAM) and S4 (p95 latency) alongside accuracy. NVIDIA's accuracy runs are on H100 or unstated hardware, its perf pages are on RTX PRO or datacenter cards, and none link latency to correctness. (4) S5 is a separate refusal counter, whereas VANTAGE silently drops non-yes/no outputs.

Where ours is weaker or merely different. (1) Our corpus is synthetic FLUX stills. VANTAGE and TAR use human-curated real footage with NVIDIA's own provenance, so we carry domain-gap risk that declared truth does not remove. (2) VANTAGE and TAR evaluate hypothesis checks on hard fixed-camera clips with no detector gate. Our task (detector-gated stills, benign vs incident, plus a risk score) is not like for like. Its best specificity among models with a published split is 76.27%, but that says nothing about whether S2 <= 5% is attainable on our task, and NVIDIA's numbers neither validate nor refute our bars. (3) Like NVIDIA, we score one operating point and have no calibration measure for the risk score. NVIDIA's own noise-floor statements (about 0.01 repeat, up to 0.05 cross-session, VANTAGE EV ±7 points) are a reminder to measure our run-to-run spread, and our Wilson intervals do not capture that spread. (4) Our S1 bar is NVIDIA's 0.85 GPU-allocation sizing rule, which is a deployment rule and not an evaluation method (the 24/16/12 GB budgets are our own arithmetic, and S1 should read GB, not GiB).

What to borrow, and what NVIDIA offers that we cannot use. Borrow the explicit scope-of-interval statement, paired and repeated runs against a measured noise floor, a recorded eval-conditions block, and benign-set sizing against the 5% bar. There is nothing to adopt from their pipeline-level accuracy method, because none is published; the one VSS accuracy statement (EVS++, about 1%) is relative and unnamed.

## 5. What to borrow for synthbench

- **State in every synthbench report what the interval does and does not cover. Our Wilson intervals cover item sampling only, not prompt, quant, decoding or frame-budget changes. Back that statement with a small prompt/decoding sensitivity check on the frozen items.**
  - Why: VANTAGE is explicit that its bootstrap intervals 'cover item sampling only', and its Limitations section says what they omit. This is the most honest part of NVIDIA's reporting, and it prevents readers from treating our Wilson bands as total uncertainty.
  - Cost: Low for the wording. Medium for the sensitivity runs (a few extra replay passes on the same items).
  - Source: VANTAGE-Bench arXiv 2609.09396 App. G.1-G.2 and Limitations [E]
- **Size the benign set against the S2 bar up front. With 0 false accepts, a Wilson upper bound at or below 5% needs at least 73 benign items (59 gives 6.1%). If we expect even 1-2 false alarms, we need several hundred benign items.**
  - Why: VANTAGE's 59 negatives cannot resolve a 5% bar, which is why NVIDIA's EV specificity intervals are 15-20 points wide. Our owner audit and benign counts should be set so S2 is decidable.
  - Cost: None for the arithmetic. Corpus cost if more benign stills must be generated.
  - Source: Computed from VANTAGE Table 7 (n_neg=59), Wilson z=1.96 [C]
- **Run any A/B (model vs model, quant vs quant, prompt vs prompt) as paired, back-to-back replays on the same frozen items. Measure repeat-run spread on an identical config and call a delta real only if it exceeds that spread. Re-run anything that matters.**
  - Why: NVIDIA's own caption harness found about 0.01 spread on identical configs, with the delta flipping sign, and up to 0.05 when REF was captured in another session. Its sign-only verdict contradicts that noise floor, which is a trap to avoid.
  - Cost: Low to medium: extra replay passes and a repeat-spread line in the report.
  - Source: skills/benchmarking/vss-evaluate-caption-accuracy/SKILL.md:118-143; scripts/score.py:299-301 [E]
- **Keep S5 as its own counter and always print answered/total next to every rate. Fix one convention (errors and unparseable outputs excluded from S2/S3 denominators but counted in S5) and apply it identically in every harness.**
  - Why: VANTAGE drops outputs with no yes/no and only prints 'Valid Predictions n/N'. NVIDIA's own harnesses disagree: legacy NAT scores judge failures as 0.0 inside the mean, while the new QA harness records None and excludes them. The same metric moves with the harness.
  - Cost: Low.
  - Source: vantage_event_verification.py:220-263 @3a428aec; vss_agents/evaluators/utils.py:272-284 and skills/benchmarking/vss-benchmark-vlm-qa/SKILL.md:154 [E]
- **Record the full eval conditions in each replay summary: model, quant and file hash, decoding (greedy/temperature), frame budget or resolution, prompt hash, hardware, and a judge model if any judge is used. Compare only against runs with matching conditions.**
  - Why: NVIDIA's QA summary.json records judge_model and model_served because those move absolute scores, and VANTAGE publishes decoding, fps, frame and pixel budgets. The same cell differs between the Cosmos 3 report and the VANTAGE paper, which shows how easily carried numbers drift.
  - Cost: Low.
  - Source: benchmark_vlm_qa.py:938; VANTAGE App. F.1-F.2 Table 12; Cosmos 3 report Table 10 vs VANTAGE Table 1 [E][C]
- **Report sensitivity and specificity (our S3 and S2) as primary, with macro F1 at most as a secondary summary. Keep the asymmetry visible per model, since NVIDIA found models fail in opposite directions.**
  - Why: VANTAGE shows Qwen3-VL-8B at 74.58 specificity / 50.96 sensitivity and CR2-8B at 57.63 / 71.15. Macro F1 conceals which way a verifier fails, which matters for our recall-first, benign-suppression split.
  - Cost: None (already how S2/S3 work); only a reporting habit.
  - Source: VANTAGE Table 7 and App. D.1 [E]
- **Fill the gap NVIDIA leaves: measure our own accuracy per quant variant (BF16 vs FP8 vs 4-bit) on the frozen corpus, for each candidate VLM, on the target consumer card. Do not assume any NVIDIA statement transfers.**
  - Why: NVIDIA benchmarks FP8/NVFP4 for speed only and warns about hallucination. The only FP8 accuracy figure is a roughly 1% delta from EVS++ with pruning on and an undefined metric.
  - Cost: Medium to high: GPU time per variant, which our bake-off already does for models.
  - Source: docs/real-time-vlm.mdx:91; docs/agent-workflow-alert-verification.mdx:159-161 [E]
- **Optional external anchor: submit the candidate VLM to the VANTAGE EV leaderboard (test inputs are public, GT withheld) to calibrate our corpus against an independent hypothesis-verification set. Treat it as a sanity check only.**
  - Why: It gives a third-party number on real fixed-camera footage. The domain is warehouse/ITS rather than home, and the tailgating overlap with Cosmos 3 training is unstated, so it cannot validate S2 or S3.
  - Cost: Medium: extraction, prompt adaptation and submission; value is low to medium.
  - Source: HF datasets/nvidia/PhysicalAI-VANTAGE-Bench README; NVIDIA/Cosmos vlmevalkit README.md:154 [E]
