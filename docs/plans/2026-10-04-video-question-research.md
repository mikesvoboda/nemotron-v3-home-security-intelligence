# Can the models process video, and would video help? Research notes (2026-10-04)

> **Status: raw, unaudited. [A] throughout.** Six investigators were dispatched in one workflow (`wf_0d9e1bb7-34e`, on `claude-sonnet-5-5`, served by the Anthropic API); all six returned just before the session ended and are reproduced below as they came back, with nothing re-verified. The owner's three questions: (1) the pipeline "only extracts stills out of the videos", is that true? (2) can these models process video directly, or is it only an image pipeline? (3) might they perform better if they could look at the video directly? Findings are fenced so a formatter cannot rewrite identifiers. This is a working note, not part of the register or the State page.

## The short answers, from the investigators' own summaries

1. **Partly true, and mostly false for the model that matters.** The detector (YOLO26) extracts stills from video with ffmpeg (every 4 s, at most 20 frames), detects on them and deletes them. The VLM path extracts nothing: a clip reaches `VlmClient._image_parts` as a path to an .mp4, which it refuses, so the event is stored `verification_failed` with a NULL score. The VLM only ever scores still JPEG/PNG files the camera uploaded, up to 4 per batch.
2. **The shipped build is image-only; the sweep's build is not, but only through frames.** llama.cpp `b7972` (the shipped pin) accepts only text, `image_url` and `input_audio`. `b11376` adds an `input_video` part (alias `video_url`) and `--video-fps` flags: it shells out to ffmpeg, decodes frames (default 4 fps, server-wide), feeds them as ordinary image chunks with a text timestamp, and merges frame pairs for Qwen projectors. It is not a native video encoder. The `ai-vlm` runtime stage installs no ffmpeg, so video does not work as built, and a 10 s clip at 4 fps is about 20K tokens against a 16,384-token slot. The Qwen3-VL, Qwen3.5, Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8 families are native-video models on the transformers/vLLM stack; Gemma 4 takes frames only.
3. **Unknown for this task; the literature does not predict it.** Temporal input helps when a task is truly temporal, but on anomaly and security tasks the measured gain is modest, saturates early and is usually smaller than the gain from telling the model what to look for; nothing found tests stills against video on home-camera incidents with a risk score. In our corpus the stranger-or-intent incidents (64 of 241, 0 to 7 hits in every arm) are mostly single-frame poses whose gap is identity and intent; only about 18-19 of 241 are time-extended by definition (loitering and part of the others).

### 2a. What the serving stack (llama.cpp llama-server + mmproj, and vLLM) can accept: video vs stills

**Answer.** The shipped build cannot take video, but the build the sweep ran can, and it still only feeds frames. The ai/vlm/Dockerfile default pin is b7972, whose OpenAI endpoint accepts only text, image_url and input_audio parts (anything else throws "unsupported content[].type"). The product's vlm_client sends only image_url parts, at most 4, and refuses .mp4 outright. The sweep's b11376 (tag = a55e952b8) adds an input_video content part (alias video_url), the --video-fps / --video-timestamp-interval / --video-ffmpeg-dir flags, and a `video` key in /props modalities. Current master's video code is the same as b11376's: master is 16 commits ahead, and the only mtmd video change is a Windows strdup fix. This is not a native video encoder. llama.cpp shells out to ffmpeg/ffprobe, decodes to RGB frames (default 4 fps, set server-wide, not per request), and feeds them as ordinary image chunks with a "Video:" prefix and a text timestamp every 5 s. Qwen-VL projectors merge adjacent frame pairs into one set of embeddings, and the header says the path is "model-agnostic... may not be accurate for some specific models". Caveats: I read source and docs only and ran nothing. The ai-vlm runtime stage installs only curl and libgomp1, so by code reading a video part would fail there with a generic 400 unless ffmpeg is added. Context cost is the binding limit: at the 16,384-token slot, a 10 s 1344x768 clip at the 4 fps default is about 20K tokens for Qwen [C] and overflows. vLLM does accept `video_url` for Qwen-VL-family models (default fps 2), but its Gemma 4 path just converts video to text plus image frames. Our repo has never sent a video to either engine, so none of this is accuracy evidence.

### Q2b: native video input per model family (cards, tech reports, mmproj headers) and usability through llama.cpp b11376

**Answer.** Qwen3-VL 8B/32B/30B-A3B, Qwen3.5 4B/9B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.8-27B are all native-video models on the transformers/vLLM stack. Each has one ViT with temporal_patch_size 2, a Qwen3VLVideoProcessor, a video token, and textual timestamps. Their cards claim hour-scale video, with a 256K native context. Every card except Qwen3.8-27B reports video benchmarks. Qwen3.8-27B claims video in prose and has a video example, but its card has no video numbers. Gemma 4 12B and 26B-A4B only claim "process videos as frames" (at most 60 s at 1 fps). Neither the cards nor the tech report give any video benchmark, and the tech report never mentions video, so for Gemma "native video" is no or unclear.

llama.cpp b11376 does have a real video path, which the shipped pin b7972 lacks entirely. The server accepts `video_url`/`input_video`, uses ffmpeg to extract frames at 4 fps, and merges frame pairs for the qwen3vl_merger projector. I read the GGUF headers of all 10 mmproj files we used. The 8 Qwen files declare qwen3vl_merger and carry both temporal slices of the patch embedding, so the same mmproj tower serves stills and video. Gemma gets no temporal merge, so its frames are independent images.

Three caveats apply. First, our ai-vlm Dockerfile runtime stage installs no ffmpeg, and the shipped LLAMA_CPP_REF is b7972, so video is not usable as built. Second, the llama.cpp timestamp text and cadence differ from the Hugging Face (HF) reference, so card benchmark numbers do not transfer. Third, those numbers are vendor-reported on public video QA benchmarks, at up to 2,048 frames and 256K context. They are not security or intent benchmarks.

Per family, 'native video / usable through llama.cpp b11376':

- Qwen3-VL 8B, 32B, 30B-A3B: yes / yes in source, conditional on ffmpeg in the image, untested.
- Qwen3.5 4B and 9B: yes / yes, same projector.
- Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: yes / yes, same projector.
- Qwen3.8-27B (all 3 quants): yes by claim, unmeasured on the card / yes, same projector.
- Gemma 4 12B: unclear (frames-as-images; card is internally inconsistent) / frames-as-images only.
- Gemma 4 26B-A4B: no (frames-as-images) / frames-as-images only.

### 3a. Published evidence on video or many ordered frames versus stills for security, anomaly and intent tasks, including Qwen-VL frame-count sensitivity and false alarms

**Answer.** The literature supports "temporal input helps when the task is truly temporal", but on anomaly and security tasks the measured gain is modest, saturates early, and is usually smaller than the gain from telling the model what to look for. (i) Helps: a holistic Qwen2.5-VL-7B on UCF-Crime went from 81.5 to 88.4 AUC when going from 32 to 256 frames (CSI-VAD). Qwen2.5-VL-3B scored about 61% on Video-MME with 2 fps frames against 42-44% from a single frame. On the purpose-built temporal benchmark TVBench, single-image GPT-4o and Gemini sit at chance (35.8% and 36.3% against 33.3% random). (ii) False alarms: I found no study showing temporal context raises false alarms in general. Zero-shot VLMs given video are conservative: precision about 1.0 with recall 0.51 at 32 frames, and under 45% accuracy on temporally distributed harm. Explicit "is there danger?" prompts drive false alarms far more than video versus still does (SPRINT: GPT-5 0.59 explicit versus 0.28 neutral; Qwen3-VL-8B 0.17 on video versus 0.18 on a first frame). (iii) Does not help or stills suffice: frame count from 8 to 256 moved AUC by only about 1 point on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, while class-aware prompts moved it from 74.5 to 89.8. Frame count from 8 to 64 moved accuracy by at most 1.7% on temporally composed harm. Many popular video benchmarks are solvable from one frame, and naive added temporal context lowered AUC in LATERN. Scope: nothing I opened tests stills against video on the same home-camera incidents with a risk-score output, or casing/loitering by VLM. The closest, SmartHome-Bench, is video-only on 2025-era models, and Paza uses 5 stills but has no single-frame ablation. So the literature cannot predict the sign or size of the video gain for this pipeline, which matches the repo's own L7 pre-registered gate ("The video gain may be zero").

### 3b - Which of our 241 incident scenarios need time, and which are decidable from one still; per-arm group data

**Answer.** Group A is 64 incidents in six scenarios: package_theft 19, loitering 9, peering_into_windows 9, trying_car_doors 9, casing_with_phone 9 and tailgating 9, with declared bands from [40,70] to [60,90]. In the declared still prompts, five of the six are drawn as an instantaneous pose (stoop and lift a parcel, hand cupped to glass, hand on a car handle, phone held up, a second person at the door-holder's shoulder), so the act is observable in one frame. What one frame cannot show is who the person is and why: resident or stranger, own car or not, placing or taking a parcel. Loitering is the exception: its definition is duration ("lingering", "not moving") [A]. My reading of the declared prompts is that about 18-19 of 241 incidents (7.5-7.9%) are time-extended by definition: loitering 9, trying_car_doors 7-8, casing 2. The repo's own figures are 45/241 = 18.7% (doc 18 calls the five 'suspicious' scenarios "the temporal ones") and 64/241 = 26.6% (ISS-086 stranger/intent). The remaining roughly 45 of the 64 are identity/intent/context gaps that time might or might not supply [A]. The data are consistent with this but do not prove it: A scores 0-7 of 64 on every one of the 13 distinct arms, 49 of 64 A items are hit by no arm, and no arm saw more than one still. Group B moves 15 to 51 of 82 but mostly through one scenario (child_alone_at_pool 0 to 17 of 19). Group C is 48 to 80 of 95. Neither rises monotonically with parameter count (rank correlation of S3 with total parameters 0.31, n=13) [C]. Caveats: n=9 per scenario gives wide intervals, the twin pairings are mine, the sweep used the shipped no-rubric prompt with an ideal detector and empty household and specialist context, and I did not view any still image.

### 3c. Testing whether video helps using only existing clip assets

**Answer.** Yes, a small test can run with no new generation, but only for the stranger/intent subset, and it can only detect a large effect. Existing assets: 164 'ready' clips (144 benign, 17 incident, 3 ambiguous). All 17 incidents are stranger/intent scenarios (loitering 5, tailgating 5, trying_car_doors 4, peering_into_windows 2, casing_with_phone 1). No threat, object-cued or package_theft clip exists. Every clip is 1344x768 H.264, 24 fps, 243 frames (10.125 s), and was made image-to-video from a source still that is already in the 450-item sweep store, so its still-only score already exists. On those 17 incident stills, 13 of 15 sweep arms score 0/17 at the floor and the other two score 1/17, so the still baseline is a floor effect. The smallest design is A' (clip frame 0, N=1), B4 and B8 (evenly spaced frames sent as 4 or 8 images) and C (native `video_url`), run on Qwen3-VL-8B Q4_K_M over 161 items (17 incident + 144 benign). A paired exact sign test on 17 clips needs at least 5 flips from 0 (one-sided p=0.031) or 6 (two-sided p=0.031), so only effects of about 30 points or more are detectable. The owner's 10-point rule from doc 18 is not testable at n=17. Arms A', B4 and B8 are feasible today, with N=8 needing a transport shim. Arm C is not runnable as-is: b11376 accepts video, but our image's runtime stage installs no ffmpeg. The estimate for all four arms is roughly 29-49 min on the 8B, and that is a computed estimate, not a measurement. A null result would not show video cannot help, because these are synthetic 10 s micro-motion clips with unaudited truth and one clip (C-clips-1-223) does not start from its source render. A positive result would not show real-camera video helps either. Three things need owner confirmation. Extracting frames from existing mp4s creates derived images, so the in-memory shim route is the safe reading of 'do not render'. Frame bursts through the product VLM were rejected in the clips design (C1) and OD-5. Bursts need a time-label scaffold that the shipped prompt lacks.
